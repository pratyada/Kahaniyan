Indian Bedtime Stories for Kids — From Krishna to Panchatantra

6 min read May 16, 2026 For NRI & Indian families

If you grew up in India, you remember Dadi's stories. Krishna stealing butter. Hanuman carrying the mountain. The clever crow and the pitcher. These weren't just entertainment — they shaped who you are.

Now you're raising kids in Canada (or the US, or UK, or UAE) — and you want them to have the same connection to these stories. But you're exhausted after work, and your child's Hindi isn't great, and the Amar Chitra Katha comics are gathering dust.

My Sleepy Tale solves this. Every night, your child hears a classic Indian story — beautifully narrated in English, with their own name woven in. No screens. Just a voice, a story, and a lesson.

Why Audio Stories Work Better Than Reading

Let's be honest: your 5-year-old doesn't read Hindi. And after a 10-hour day in Mississauga, you don't have the energy to narrate a 15-minute story from memory.

My Sleepy Tale handles it. One tap. The story plays in a warm English narrator's voice — with your child's name, your family members, and the cultural setting intact. They hear "Aarav" not "the boy." They hear "Dadi" not "grandmother."

"My kids are growing up in Brampton and I was worried they'd lose connection to our stories. Now every night they ask for 'the Krishna story' or 'the monkey one.' They're learning values without even knowing it."

Not Just Hindu — All Indian Traditions

India isn't one religion. My Sleepy Tale has stories from:

  • Hindu — Krishna, Rama, Hanuman, Panchatantra, Akbar-Birbal
  • Sikh — Guru Nanak's Langar, Bhai Kanhaiya (the water carrier)
  • Jain — Mahavir and the Ants, the Spider's Web
  • Buddhist — Buddha and the Swan, the Elephant's Patience
  • Muslim — Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) and the Camel, the Thirsty Dog

Whatever your family's tradition — or if you want your child to know ALL of India's stories — it's all here.
